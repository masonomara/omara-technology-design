## Scope Labs — Deep Analysis

### What They Do Well

**Engagement-type framing, not skill-type listing.**
This is the most important thing Scope does. They don't have a page called "What We Can Do." They have pages called `/startup`, `/enterprise`, and `/ai` — each one is a conversation with a specific person in a specific situation. The reader arrives and immediately knows if they're in the right place. This is powerful because it filters and qualifies at the same time. Their service list isn't "here's what we know how to do" — it's "here's who we are for you, specifically."

**Empathy-first opening.**
The startup page opens with: "We've been founders. We've hired too early. Shipped too late. Overbuilt. Under-scoped. Scope exists to prevent those mistakes." That's real. It's specific. It names the exact pain points that make a founder wince. Before they sell anything, they prove they understand the problem from the inside. The reader feels seen before they feel sold to.

**Outcome language anchored to specifics.**
They don't say "we build great products." They say "3 months to revenue," "$8M+ Raised," "50k+ users." Every soft claim gets a hard number somewhere nearby. It builds credibility without feeling like boasting because the numbers are attached to real case studies, not floating in the hero.

**The "Both" answer to "either/or" questions.**
Their FAQ answer to "Do you operate as a full team or augment ours?" is just: "Both." That's a perfect answer. It respects the reader's intelligence, closes a common objection, and positions Scope as flexible. Simple, direct, done.

**Transition planning built into the pitch.**
They say services are "built to transition smoothly or scale long-term without creating dependency." This is smart — it preemptively addresses the fear that hiring an agency creates lock-in. Mentioning it proactively builds trust.

---

### What's Robotic and Pretentious

**The metrics grid at the top.**
"$30M+ raised. $350M+ valuation. 1M+ users. 14wks avg launch." This is the first thing after the hero and it immediately feels like a pitch deck. It interrupts the human voice they just established. Numbers in that format read as chest-thumping, not evidence.

**MBA-speak bleeding through.**
"Outcomes-driven process tied to measurable business signals." "Operational leverage." "Compound systems." "Cross-departmental buy-in." These phrases are technically coherent but they don't sound like a person talking. They sound like someone who read too many VC memos. It makes the reader work harder without giving them more meaning. Real people don't talk like this.

**The comparison diagrams.**
The "WITHOUT SCOPE / WITH SCOPE" visual timelines are the most salesy thing on the page. They feel like something out of a SaaS landing page template from 2019. It undermines the "we've been founders" credibility they built in the previous section.

**The enterprise page especially.**
The enterprise voice loses the warmth entirely: "Maps constraints across teams/workflows/tooling. Produces 90-day action plan with prioritized opportunities. Establishes KPI framework and implementation roadmap." This is consultant-speak at its most impenetrable. Every sentence is a noun phrase. Nothing breathes. It reads like a deliverables list, not a pitch from a partner who gets it.

**The taglines feel written, not spoken.**
"Your technical cofounder." "Your execution partner." These are clever in a headline-writing kind of way, but they ring slightly hollow because they're abstract positioning rather than something that follows from a real point of view. They tell you what Scope wants to be rather than what they actually are.

## Self Aware Studio — Deep Analysis

### What They Do Well

**Voice that actually sounds like a human.**
"We do a lot of listening in this phase" is one of the best lines on their entire site. It's simple, warm, and it communicates a real working style in seven words. That's harder to write than it looks. Their whole process description reads like someone is talking to you, not performing for you.

**The contact form is the best thing on the page.**
The form starts with: "Dear Self Aware, My name is (YOUR FULL NAME) and I am (YOUR ROLE) at (YOUR ORGANIZATION). We're looking for..." This is genuinely clever. It writes the client's first email for them. It lowers the activation energy of reaching out. It also communicates personality — Self Aware clearly has a real relationship with their clients from the first interaction.

**"We're all about style and substance."**
This line is doing a lot of work. It's casual, confident, and it positions their values without being preachy. It's a flag planted in the ground. You know what they believe in.

**Transparent pricing.**
"$40,000 to $175,000 across 6 to 25 weeks." This is brave and it builds enormous trust. Most agencies dance around pricing because they think transparency loses deals. Self Aware knows it wins the right deals and filters out the wrong ones.

**"Keys to your shiny new website 🔑"**
This is the timeline outcome label for a development engagement. It's tiny but it's perfect. It's the one moment of levity in an otherwise practical document. It shows personality without sacrificing professionalism. It makes the deliverable feel like something worth celebrating rather than a transaction closing.

**They show the process, not just the service.**
Their "Sample Timeline" section with milestones is excellent. It tells the client exactly what they'll be doing and when. It removes uncertainty. Most agencies hide this information because they're afraid of commitment — Self Aware leans into it and it builds confidence.

---

### What's Missing

**They don't explain why them.**
The process page is clear about what they do but never directly says why they're the right choice. There's no perspective, no point of view, no "here's what most agencies get wrong." They describe their process but don't advocate for it. A little more conviction — like Scope's "we've been there" framing — would strengthen this.

**No social proof.**
No client names, no project outcomes, no testimonials. For a studio that works at $40k-$175k, this is a gap. The work likely speaks for itself on the portfolio, but a line or two anchoring the process description to real results would sharpen the pitch.

**The hero is generic.**
"Our goal for each project is client satisfaction, which we achieve through an efficient and pragmatic approach that is the result of years of hands-on experience..." This is table-stakes agency copy. Anyone could say this. It doesn't sound like Self Aware — it sounds like a disclaimer. The rest of the page is so much better.

## O'Mara Technology — Current State Assessment

### What's There

Five service categories with comma-separated capability lists:
1. Product design strategy (monthly/extended)
2. Product design services (ad-hoc)
3. Technical development strategy (monthly/extended)
4. Technical development services (ad-hoc)
5. Supplementary services (through partners)

Subtitle: "Available for product design and technical development strategy and services. Pricing determined by hourly rate."

### What's Working

The categories are logically correct. The strategy/services split along the engagement-length axis is smart — it reflects how this actually works. The items within each category are specific and credible. "MCP Server Design," "AI & RAG Architecture," "Row Level Security" — these signal real technical depth without being bullshit.

### What's Not Working

**No hook, no voice, no why.**
The subtitle is pure information without any personality. It reads like metadata. There's no line that makes you want to keep reading or that communicates who O'Mara Technology is.

**"Pricing determined by hourly rate" is a dead end.**
This answers a question (how do you price?) while creating new anxiety (how much per hour? how many hours?). It's also the last thing a prospect reads, which means the page ends on uncertainty rather than invitation.

**The category naming is confusing from the outside.**
"Product design strategy" vs. "Product design services" — the distinction between these two isn't obvious to a client who doesn't already understand the engagement model. From the inside this makes sense. From the outside it raises questions.

**No framing of the engagement experience.**
The page tells you what you get but not what it's like to work with O'Mara. Is it collaborative? What does a week look like? What's the rhythm? Self Aware's process page answers all of this. The current page doesn't attempt to.

**No call to action.**
The page ends. There's a footer contact section via `FooterContact`, but the services page itself doesn't drive toward anything. It presents and then goes quiet.

## The Synthesis — What to Build

### The Core Tension

Scope is professional and credible but writes for the pitch. Self Aware is warm and human but undersells their perspective. O'Mara Technology needs to do what neither does perfectly: **sound like a real person who knows their craft and is genuinely trying to help the right client find them.**

### The Right Frame: Engagements, Not Services

The current page organizes around capability types (design, development). The rewrite should organize around **engagement types** — the way Scope does with startup/enterprise, but more honest and less salesy. O'Mara has two natural engagement modes that already exist in the data:

**Ongoing / Strategic**
Monthly or extended relationships. Design strategy + technical strategy. The client gets a thinking partner, not just an executor. These are engagements where O'Mara is embedded in the product decisions, not just delivering to a spec.

**Project / Build**
Defined scope. Design services + development services. The client has a clear thing that needs to be built or shipped. O'Mara comes in, does the work, delivers it.

Everything currently in "Supplementary services" (Shopify, social, SEO, content) can stay as a supporting note — these exist and are available through trusted collaborators, but they aren't the lead.

### Voice Direction

From Self Aware: conversational, specific, concrete. "We do a lot of listening" energy. Not formal, not stiff.

From Scope: direct, confident, outcome-aware. Willing to say "here's who this is for" and "here's who it isn't."

From O'Mara: honest. The site already has a real voice. The services page just needs to meet that standard. Don't inflate. Don't perform. Don't pretend this is a firm of 40 people if it isn't.

### Page Structure Proposal

**1. Opening / Positioning (2–3 sentences)**
Not a tagline. An honest, specific statement about what O'Mara Technology is and what kind of client/project is a fit. Self Aware's process page intro is a reference point — it's just too generic. This should be specific enough to be surprising.

**2. Two Engagement Types (with their own description and item lists)**

*Strategic Engagements* — Monthly or extended. Who this is for, what the rhythm is, what's included. Use the existing strategy items.

*Project Engagements* — Scoped work. What this looks like in practice, what gets delivered. Use the existing service items.

Each engagement type needs: a short honest description in O'Mara's voice, followed by the capability list (the current items are good, they just need framing).

**3. How It Works (short)**
Self Aware's process section energy — brief, concrete, specific. Not a diagram. Not a comparison chart. Just: this is what it looks like in practice. What are the touchpoints? What does a client need to bring? What does O'Mara bring?

**4. Pricing (honest)**
Not a number necessarily, but more transparent than "hourly rate." Something like what Self Aware does — give enough context that a client can self-qualify. Or keep it honest: hourly, with a note on how that works across engagement types.

**5. CTA (clear)**
A single, direct invitation. FooterContact handles this currently — make sure the page earns it by the time the reader gets there.

## What to Avoid

- Jargon for the sake of credibility ("measurable business signals," "operational leverage")
- Generic agency copy ("our goal is client satisfaction")
- Deflating calls to action ("pricing determined by hourly rate")
- Hiding the engagement model — tell people what working together actually looks like
