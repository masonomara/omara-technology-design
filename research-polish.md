_April 2026_

## 1. Competitor Analysis

### Scope Labs — scopelabs.com

**What they do well**
They speak the language of their buyers. Founders and CTOs read "MVP builds and sprints to reach launch, traction, and growth" and immediately know what they're buying. The metrics ($30M raised, $350M+ client valuation, 14 weeks avg to launch) answer the question every client has before asking it: _has anyone trusted you with real money?_ The testimonials are specific and credible — not "great team to work with" but "technical co-founder...scaled and raised over $8mm."

They also commit to a positioning. They're not a vendor. They're a partner with founder DNA. That's a real differentiator from the generic agency.

**What they do badly**
It reads like a pitch deck, not a conversation. The language is airtight and bloodless. "Operationalize AI across your business with systems your team can actually use" — every word is calculated, nothing feels earned. The "work family" testimonial language sits awkwardly against the corporate framing.

They're also covering too much ground. Startups, enterprise, AI, incubator — it starts to feel like they'll say yes to anything. The positioning dilutes itself.

**Takeaway for O'Mara:** Borrow their instinct to answer the buyer's questions before they're asked. Don't borrow the tone. They sound like they're trying to convince you. We should sound like we already know.

---

### Self Aware Studio — selfaware.studio

**What they do well**
They let the work carry the weight. Project descriptions like "Fun and approachable new website(s) for Philly's cult favorite sandwich joint" do more positioning than three paragraphs of credentials. You understand the studio's sensibility instantly from the work they take on and how they describe it.

Their language is specific without being precious. "Two Sites in One," "One unified system" — these aren't creative writing, they're just accurate. The specificity earns trust. It shows they understood what they were solving.

The Awwwards/Typewolf mentions are woven in naturally, not foregrounded. Credibility through context, not chest-beating.

**What they do badly**
Their homepage is extremely minimal — "Self Aware is a design and technology studio that brings brands to life on the web" is actually a weak headline for how strong their work is. The studio description tells you almost nothing about who they're for or what makes them different from the 500 other Webflow/Framer studios.

There's also very little personality on the macro level. The micro-copy on individual projects is charming. The studio-level identity feels underdeveloped.

**Takeaway for O'Mara:** Borrow their commitment to specificity. A one-line project description that's genuinely accurate beats a paragraph of vague benefits every time. Also: their studio description is weak, and ours is stronger. Don't water it down trying to sound like them.

## 2. Current Site Copy Analysis

### What's working

**The project descriptions are the strongest copy on the site.** "Crew placement platform for the yachting industry — launched with 500 users at Fort Lauderdale." "Search engine sales went from 7% to 49% of revenue within a month of launch." These are exactly right. Specific, outcome-focused, one sentence. They read like someone who was actually there.

**The About page ethos statement is good.** "Everything changes faster than anyone can track. Tools, platforms, markets — constantly. What holds up is timeless thinking and creativity that meets the moment." This is a genuine belief, not a tagline manufactured in a brainstorm. It holds up.

**The services capabilities descriptions have real conviction.** "Built to ship, not just to demo." "The creative work stays here — not outsourced, not templated." "This is where most ideas should stop — and the ones that shouldn't come out the other side with a real direction." These earn credibility.

**The contact form copy is genuinely good.** The prose-sentence format ("My name is **_ and I'm reaching out from _**") is a smart, human design choice that differentiates from every generic contact form.

---

### What needs work

**"Everything changes" is pulling double duty.** It opens the About page and opens the Services page. Using the same anchoring thought on two different pages makes both feel weaker. It's the central thesis of the brand — it should live in one place and be earned, not restated.

**The About page intro is redundant with itself.** "O'Mara Technology is Mason O'Mara" is on the About page. The Bio section at the bottom of the same page also introduces Mason O'Mara in the third person, with similar information. The page introduces him twice. That's one introduction too many, and it makes the page feel unresolved — like two drafts merged without a final pass.

**The Services page "How It Works" section is long.** It's the strongest writing on the site and also the most over-written. The section about Discovery is four sentences when it should be two. The section about the loop and AI compacting development cycles is genuinely interesting but buries itself in qualifications. Strunk & White: omit needless words. The insight is real. The delivery dilutes it.

**The 404 page reads as unfinished.** The buttons ("FOR OPPORTUNITIES TO WORK WITH O.T.D. PLEASE CLICK HERE") feel like a placeholder joke that was left in production. Either commit to making the 404 page a genuinely fun experience, or make it clean and functional.

**The marquee text is the weakest copy on the site.** "Everything changes → Be creative → Explore timeless work" doesn't reflect the voice anywhere else on the site. "Be creative" especially sounds like clipart. This should either be cut or replaced with something that actually sounds like the brand.

**Tone inconsistency between pages.** The About page is first-person plural ("We work alongside you"). The capabilities grid uses second-person direction ("Built to ship"). The Bio section is third-person ("He's shipped iOS and Android apps"). The Services page mixes all three. Pick a register per context and stay in it.

---

### Voice calibration

The current voice is: direct, confident, slightly raw, occasionally over-explains.

The target voice is: direct, specific, no qualification. Confident without performing confidence. Reads fast. Every sentence earns its place.

The best sentences on the site already do this. The work is cutting the ones that don't and making the remaining ones consistent.

## 3. Copy Recommendations by Page

### About Page

**Issues:** Introduces Mason twice. Opening ethos and opening of Services page are the same move. Tone shifts between sections.

**Fixes:**

- Keep the ethos statement as the opener — it's earned.
- Cut the redundant bio. The Bio section at the bottom should be the one introduction, either in first or third person — not both. Merge into one clear section.
- The "How We Work" engagement descriptions (Strategic / Project) are clean. Keep them.
- The capabilities grid descriptions are strong. Minor cleanup only — cut any line that could be on a competitor's site without changing.

---

### Services Page

**Issues:** Opens with a near-duplicate of the About intro. "How It Works" is the longest section and could be 40% shorter. Pricing section is clear and should stay mostly as-is.

**Fixes:**

- Replace the "Everything changes fast" opener with something that leads directly into what services are, not a restatement of brand philosophy. That work is done by About.
- Cut the How It Works section ruthlessly. Keep the insight about the loop. Keep the point about discovery being where most ideas stop. Cut every sentence that's explaining what the previous sentence already said.
- The timeline tables are clean and useful. Keep them.

---

### Work Page

**Issues:** The project one-liners are the best copy on the site. No major changes needed.

**Fixes:**

- Review for consistency of format: every card should be one sentence, past tense or present-outcome tense. A few cards shift to present tense describing the project ("A two-sided marketplace...") — standardize.
- Make sure every card has a concrete result or specific detail. A few don't ("Brand, tools, and operations for a creative studio" has no hook).

---

### Contact Page

**Issues:** Minimal. The form design is good.

**Fixes:**

- The fallback line ("Contact forms not your thing? Email us at info@omaratechnology.com") is fine but "not your thing" is slightly off-brand — a bit casual in a way nothing else on the site is. Consider: "Prefer email? info@omaratechnology.com."

---

### 404 Page

**Issues:** The joke buttons feel unresolved — not funny enough to be a bit, not clean enough to be functional.

**Fixes:** Either go all-in with the joke (make the buttons actually lead somewhere interesting/funny) or replace with a simple, clean 404 with a link home. Currently it's in-between territory that serves neither.

---

### Marquee

**Issues:** "Be creative" is generic in a way nothing else on the site is.

**Fix:** Replace with text that sounds like the brand. Options that fit the voice:

- "Ship things worth using"
- "Timeless thinking, then build"
- "Design. Build. Launch."

Or cut the middle phrase and rotate the two that are already good.

## 4. Codebase Readability — Issues & Fixes

### Critical — Fix these

**1. NAV_LINKS is defined twice.**
`Header.tsx` and `Footer.tsx` both define the same navigation array independently. Extract to `src/app/lib/constants.ts` and import from both.

**2. Email address is hardcoded in 4+ places.**
`info@omaratechnology.com` appears in `Project.tsx`, `Footer.tsx`, the contact page, and possibly elsewhere. Should be one constant imported everywhere.

**3. `ServiceCard.tsx` appears unused.**
`ServicesSection.tsx` reads from services JSON directly. `ServiceCard.tsx` either needs to be wired up or deleted. Dead components in a codebase erode trust that the code is maintained.

---

### High priority — simplify these

**4. `Game.tsx` is 740+ lines with mixed concerns.**
This is the hardest thing to explain to the next person who touches this code. It has:

- UI state management
- Direct DOM manipulation
- Supabase queries
- Score calculation logic
- Enemy positioning algorithm

It doesn't need to be perfect, but it should be split into at minimum:

- A `useGameState` hook (score, accuracy, timing logic)
- A `useLeaderboard` hook (Supabase reads/writes)
- The component itself (rendering, user interaction)

**5. `Game.module.css` is 750+ lines.**
A CSS file this long in a module system is a red flag. Leaderboard styles, game board styles, and hand animation styles are all cohabitating. Split into logical sections with clear headers at minimum. Consider splitting the leaderboard into its own component with its own CSS module.

**6. `fadeIn` and `fadeInButton` in `motion.ts` are nearly identical.**
The only difference is the offset distance (20px vs 12px). These should be one function with an optional `size` parameter, or one should be deprecated. Five variants in motion.ts is exactly the right number per CLAUDE.md — but two of them being near-duplicates wastes one of the five slots.

---

### Medium priority — clean these up

**7. Hardcoded `rgba(137, 24, 16, X)` values in components.**
`CursorFollower.tsx` and possibly others reference the primary color as a raw rgba value instead of `var(--color-primary)`. This means a color change in `globals.css` won't propagate everywhere. Audit and replace with CSS variables.

**8. `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` in Project page without visible sanitization.**
The project markdown renders via `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`. This is fine for static markdown files you control. Add a comment in the code explaining that this is safe because the content source is controlled (it will confuse the next person who sees it otherwise).

**9. Base64-encoded bad words array in contact page.**
This is security through obscurity and it doesn't work — anyone who reads the source can decode it in two seconds. It doesn't need to be here at all if the validation is happening server-side. If it's client-side validation, it should just be a regular array. The obscured encoding adds complexity with no security benefit.

**10. `viewport` configuration appears in multiple places.**
Viewport meta is set in both `layout.tsx` and `next.config.ts`. This creates a maintenance issue — which one wins? One should own it.

---

### Low priority — good to address

**11. `ProjectCard.tsx` vs inline cards.**
There's both a `ProjectCard.tsx` component and inline card rendering in `ProjectsSection.tsx`. Verify which is actually used and delete the unused one.

**12. `getThumbnail()` and `getImages()` in `content.ts` are nearly identical.**
Both search the same directories with similar extension logic. A shared `getProjectFiles(type)` utility would be cleaner and would be the obvious place to add a third file type if needed later.

**13. Animation delay magic numbers lack context.**
Values like `0.12`, `0.35`, `0.4` in motion variant calls are arbitrary without comments. Either extract to named constants (`ANIMATION_DELAY_SHORT = 0.12`) or add a comment explaining the cadence logic.

---

## 5. Summary

**Copy:** The voice is already good. The work is editing, not rewriting. Biggest fix: get "Everything changes" off the Services page, consolidate the two Mason O'Mara introductions on About, cut the How It Works section by 40%, and replace the marquee middle phrase.

**Code:** The codebase is in decent shape architecturally. The main readability problems are Game.tsx's size and mixed concerns, the duplicated NAV_LINKS, and the hardcoded email addresses. Fix those three and the next person reading this code will have a much easier time.

**Voice targets:**

- Scope Labs taught us: answer the buyer's question before they ask. Results, specificity, proof.
- Self Aware taught us: let the work speak. Short project descriptions that are genuinely accurate beat vague benefits.
- The site already does this in the best moments. Make the worst moments match the best ones.
